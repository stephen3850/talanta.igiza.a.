3. Executive Summary
Talanta Stadium is a nationally significant sports venue. The opportunity proposed here is to build a joined-up digital layer around
event discovery, ticket sales, payment confirmation, gate validation, workforce accreditation, event control and financial
reconciliation. The platform should make public-facing services easier to use while giving authorised operators a clearer, auditable
view of what is happening during an event.
The proposed Integrated Talanta Stadium Digital Infrastructure (ITSDI) is modular. The client can approve a core ticketing and
reporting release first, then add approved integrations and more advanced capabilities after proving security, accessibility and
operational readiness. This phased approach avoids making the whole programme dependent on unverified hardware or
biometric assumptions.
Core outcomes proposed
• One official public portal for approved fixtures, ticket categories, venue guidance and customer support.
• Secure ticket issuance, payment-status tracking, refund handling and event-level reconciliation.
• Gate-validation workflows with offline contingency procedures and clear exception handling.
• Role-based staff and contractor accreditation, rosters, attendance and expiry tracking.
• A command dashboard for authorised operators, with occupancy indicators, incidents, gate health and finance summaries.
• Privacy-conscious design, cybersecurity controls, auditable administrator actions and a documented handover.
Decision sought
Approve a discovery and feasibility stage to confirm the operator's requirements, current systems, event
schedule, hardware inventory, data-protection obligations, procurement route and final business case. A
detailed fixed scope and binding commercial offer should follow only when these facts are established.

7. Public Website and Mobile Experience
Illustrative visual concept generated for this proposal. It is not an existing official website or approved brand design. The final visual identity must be
approved by the stadium operator.
The website should be the official digital entry point for fans. It should load quickly on common Kenyan mobile devices, remain
usable on slower networks, and communicate the event rules and ticket terms before payment.
• Home: official notices, fixtures, venue information, ticket calls to action and service alerts.
• Event page: date/time, ticket categories, accessible seating details, entry rules, prohibited items, transport and refund policy.
• Booking: seat/zone availability, clear total price, order summary, expiry timer and safe payment handoff.
• Ticket wallet: secure ticket reference, QR where approved, order status, resend/recovery and customer support.
• Help centre: frequently asked questions, fraud warnings, lost-ticket guidance, refunds and escalation contact.
• Administration: approved event publishing, price/category configuration, inventory controls and auditable changes.
Design standards
Target WCAG 2.2 AA where applicable, responsive layouts, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, readable contrast,
accessible error messages and plain-language content. Support both smartphone and desktop journeys; consider SMS/USSD or
staffed assistance only after cost, accessibility and operational feasibility are confirmed.

9. High-Level System Architecture
Architecture concept
• Public channel: browser-based website, mobile web and optional notifications.
• API layer: authentication, authorisation, rate limiting, input validation and central audit events.
• Business services: ticket inventory, payments, ticket validation, accreditation, workforce, reports and notifications.
• Data layer: relational transactional data, controlled document/object storage, audit log store and backups.
• Venue edge: locally available validation data only where approved; synchronisation and documented offline rules.
• External services: payment provider and any government identity services only after formal approval and interface confirmation.
The design should favour clear service boundaries and a maintainable deployment over unnecessary microservices. The final
choice between a modular monolith and distributed services should be based on scale, team capability, support costs and
integration requirements.

12. Gate Access, Ticket Validation and Crowd Operations
Gate validation must be fast, observable and safe. The precise validation method depends on existing devices and the operator's
security model. A standard QR or NFC credential may be sufficient for many ticket classes; restricted zones may require stronger
staff accreditation controls.
Proposed validation sequence
• Scanner reads the ticket token and verifies signature/status through the approved validation service.
• System checks event, entry window, ticket category, revocation and prior-use rules.
• Approved result returns clear accept/reject/exception status with minimal personal data displayed.
• A successful entry is recorded with gate/device, event, timestamp and ticket reference.
• An exception routes to a trained supervisor rather than forcing an unsafe or discriminatory decision.
• If network connectivity is lost, operators follow a rehearsed, documented fallback with reconciliation afterwards.
Crowd and occupancy indicators
Dashboards may display counts and trends from validated scans and other approved sources. Scan counts are not automatically
equal to physical occupancy: re-entry, staff, exits, scanning failures and emergency procedures must be accounted for. Any
occupancy estimate must be labelled with its source and limitations.
Emergency behaviour
The software must never override the venue's approved life-safety design. Emergency exits, fail-safe gate
behaviour and evacuation procedures must be engineered and approved by qualified specialists and tested
with the venue operator. The proposal does not assume that every optical barrier can or should automatically
behave identically during every emergency.

13. Identity Verification and Optional Biometrics
Biometric facial recognition is a sensitive, high-impact option—not a default requirement. It should be considered only if the
stadium operator demonstrates a specific need that cannot be reasonably met by less intrusive methods, and after legal, privacy,
accuracy, bias, accessibility and security review.
Required decision gates before any biometric deployment
• Define the precise purpose and lawful basis; obtain legal and data-protection review.
• Complete a Data Protection Impact Assessment where required and consult the relevant authority if advised.
• Assess whether QR/NFC credentials, visual ID checks or staff accreditation can achieve the purpose with less risk.
• Define enrollment, consent where applicable, retention, deletion, access, breach handling and vendor restrictions.
• Test false acceptance/rejection, demographic performance, lighting, face coverings and environmental conditions.
• Provide an accessible, staffed non-biometric alternative and an appeal process for failed matches.
• Prohibit secondary use or surveillance expansion without a new approved assessment and lawful authority.
No performance figures in this proposal should be treated as a vendor-certified biometric guarantee. Any
accuracy or speed target must be specified, independently tested in representative conditions and contractually
defined before deployment.

27. Seat Mapping, Ticket Categories and Inventory Rules
Seat maps shown here are conceptual zones, not a surveyed Talanta Stadium seating plan. Production inventory must be based on approved seating plans
and event-specific configuration.
Inventory rules to implement
• Create event-specific inventory; never assume every physical seat is saleable for every event.
• Represent seats, blocks, standing zones, accessible allocations, restricted seats and sponsor holds separately.
• Use transactional locking or equivalent concurrency control to prevent overselling under high traffic.
• Apply temporary holds only for a defined time and release expired holds automatically.
• Record every manual allocation, price change, refund, complimentary ticket and inventory override in an audit trail.
• Support staged releases of ticket allocations where approved, without exposing restricted inventory.
• Offer a clear seat/zone description and accessible alternative when a particular section is unavailable.
Event configuration controls
Only authorised event managers should publish fixtures, set sale windows, change prices, open or close categories and cancel an
event. High-impact changes should require a second approver. The system should preserve a versioned record of the prior
configuration.
